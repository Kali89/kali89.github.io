Every year my wife takes on the burden of buying the children's Christmas
presents and every year she gets frustrated that she's the one doing it all.<!--more-->
Every year, I say _"I'm happy to do it"_ and every year she doesn't like the
sound of that. This year though, she finally called my bluff!
We're having a daddy Christmas this year, and I couldn't be more excited.

All the relatives are finding themselves redirected to me, and all of the
pressure now falls on my (very inexperienced) head. I spent a bit of time
talking to each of the children to find out what they want and I feel like I
have to share this, because they are apparently all certifiably insane.

## 7-year-old boy

He's become a little football mad recently, so my guesses were football
Top Trumps, football stickers/cards, a FIFA game of some sort, a football,
maybe some tennis/badminton lessons, and a trip to a Brazilian meat restaurant.

Instead, he asked for:

- Leg armour
- A book of photos of his favourite people that he can look at and make him happy
- Clothes can work as a present, but not as the main item. A shark book and toy
    can come with a shark t-shirt. But no shark t-shirt by itself. And not sharks — he doesn't like them any more.
- A clone of himself that can sneak up behind people and trick them, and play with his brother for him.


What the actual fuck?

He's compounded matters by saying that he's not going to write his Santa list
until the 24th December because he doesn't want to lose it before then.

## 4-year-old girl

A much easier prospect — if you put her in a shop with 10 items, she'd
absolutely love 9 of them. I'd guessed cuddly toys, a scarf/hat/glove set,
clothes, and art kits.

She wants:

- A pink astronaut outfit
- A dragon costume
- Zoo animals
- A cuddly chair
- A clipboard
- A Labubu

A cuddly chair and clipboard?

If we're being generous, you could argue I broadly had the right categories.
What I already know is that _whatever_ I get her, she'll have some
sort of tantrum that _something_ isn't right.

I promise I do know my own children. As much as anybody can know a 4-year-old girl.

## 1-year-old boy

He likes boxes and pans and making a mess and shouting
"fish" at the TV and shouting at animals and shouting at his siblings and
shouting. I actually sketched out:

- Loud metallic items
- (Indestructible) Books with flaps
- Light up electronic nonsense

The honest answer is that he doesn't know that it's Christmas and he'll just
want whatever his siblings are currently holding. He'll also enjoy the boxes
and wrapping paper. If it were up to me, we'd just wrap up some of the bigger
ones' old toys. Now, _technically_, it is up to me.

## Conclusion

Nabeel Qureshi wrote that [great poems are surprising](https://nabeelqu.substack.com/p/what-makes-art-great) — if you can predict the next word, it isn’t adding much. 
My wife and I know our children better than anybody, and it's so
much fun to see just how bad we are at predicting what they're going to say.
What wonderful little poems they are.
