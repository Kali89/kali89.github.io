It looks like the Riemann Hypothesis is gone.<!--more--> I'm surprised by how
much the news has shaken me. I don't feel awe or happiness — just a deep
sadness and fear for the future. It
feels like a cloud has been pulled over the future — the next decade has gone
from feeling like an exciting step into the future, to feeling like a blind
step onto a space elevator.

I'm not a mathematician. It's not like **I** was going to solve the Riemann
Hypothesis. I can barely even understand it. But I did study mathematics. I do
know the reverence with which it was held. The shock of all LLMs trained on
data _before_ November 2026 makes it plain how big of a step this is.

There are positive visions for an AI-led future. I've been interested in the
idea of immortality (life expectancy rising faster than 1 year per year,
longevity escape velocity) since
I was a teenager, and I feel like that possibility has just taken a big leap
forward. It might come too late for me, but my children could be the first
generation to not know death-by-aging. Maybe that should be exciting, but for
now it just feels baffling.

There are immediate economic concerns — will AI put me out of a job? I've saved
well over the years and am in a better position than most when it comes to
economic uncertainty. But I'd be lying if I said this wasn't a concern. I need
to work for 5 more years in order to be 'financially independent' at current
saving rates/expenses. At the moment I'd say there's a good chance of me being
able to find similar work for the next 5 years, but given the current
rate-of-change, it's hard to estimate anything that far in advance with too
much certainty.

What I'm really struggling with is the purpose of humanity. Should children
learn mathematics? We teach them mathematics, not because they are going to
solve the Riemann Hypothesis, but because being mathematically literate is a
key part of life. The skills you learn alongside maths (ability to focus on a problem, applying algorithmic processes, operating within a set of axioms, etc.) are more valuable than the mathematical learning itself. As AI agents handle more things for us, are those skills still as valuable? And if not, what skills are?

It's all well and good to live forever, but we have to have a 'why'. Why are we
here? What do we fill our time with? Is a life spent hiking and swimming and
painting a life well spent? As is a parent's prerogative, I worry less for
myself and more for my children. 

I have worked and struggled and learned and
know the sting of defeat and the elation of a problem solved. I can find
purpose and meaning in learning, and writing silly little internet blogs. Maybe
I need more confidence in my children — that whatever frightening new reality
they graduate into, for them will be normal. They'll view the pre-AI world in
the same way I view the pre-digital world.

I believe in my children and I believe in my parenting. But the move from the
real world to the digital world feels like a small jump, compared to the
massive chasm that has been hinted at over the last few weeks. I'll raise my
kids as best I can according to what I've learned in my life so far, but I
worry that their world will be so different to mine that what I can teach won't
be enough.
