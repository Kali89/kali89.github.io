My baby boy isn't really a baby any more, at the ripe old age of 13
months.<!--more--> The thing is, I'm not so sure he's a toddler either. It's
the lack of _toddling_ that does it. He can stand while holding onto something.
Actually, he can stand **without** holding onto something - it's just that he
doesn't _think_ that he can. Sometimes we trick him by giving him something to
hold, and in the few seconds after he has grabbed it (but before he realises what he's
doing) he stands proud and free.

Still no words, either. Lots of shouts that could very easily become words.
It's going to be a short loud word that he gets the hang of - our only hope is
that it isn't "gun". My 7-year-old knows this, and so is pretty determined that
it should be "gun".

No words and no walking (or even real standing), but he's a speed-demon when it
comes to crawling and to climbing. Who would have thought that somebody who
couldn't stand could scale a ladder, then go over the top and head first down a
slide? Relatedly, he's also got the first of what I'm sure
will be many head bumps. The child has no fear, apart from, apparently, when it
comes to standing without holding onto anything.

He's nailed clapping and waving, and is a dab hand at pointing too. Drumming,
both with hands and accessories. His favourite is the pan drawer, which he can
open and close, fuck up, and then smash the pans off of the floor/wall for
extra noise.

As of a few days ago, he dropped down to being a one-nap baby and he's so much
happier. It's a real pain when he only does a 45 minute nap, but we no longer
spend hours on end trying to squeeze in a second nap that he doesn't want to
have. I reminded myself when we had this child to not stress too much about
doing things _right_ and to just go with whatever works for them. Then I went
ahead and forgot it all over again. When you remember that you can just do what
works for your child and then it works...well, why can't I remember that?

He's got two teeth now, and occasionally is irrationally angry...no, wait. He's
**always** irrational, often angry, and very often irrationally angry. However,
we often attribute some of this anger to his teeth and the pain of growing new
ones. Maybe it's some other pain, or just the general anguish of existing in a
world where nobody can understand you. But it's easier to say "are your teeth
hurting?" and to give him Calpol.

He's developing cheekiness and craziness. Sometimes when he starts to get tired
and he's had a meal, he'll just crawl around the kitchen table shrieking. I
_think_ he's imitating his older siblings who like to run and do the same
thing. He loves a wrestle too, but his only move is to crawl up to me and then
dip his head at me. That's my cue to flip him upside down and generally tackle
him. Successfully flipped, he crawls away, and then crawls back at me to repeat
the process.

He's a lot of fun if he's all you're dealing with and you've got energy. He's a
lot less fun if you're trying to achieve something and he woke up at 5:30am and
wants to post electronics in the toilet while you're trying to wee.
